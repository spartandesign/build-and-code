Reference · Your path to the competition team

From EXP to the V5 Team

What this is: a map between what you're building here on VEX EXP and what the competition teams run on VEX V5 with our team's SpartanDrive library. This course is the on-ramp: the league squads start here, and the same skills carry into the qualifier squad's competition robots. Almost nothing you've learned has to be thrown away — a few commands get a new name and the place you type your code changes. This page shows the whole correspondence so that when you step onto the team, it already feels familiar.

On this page: a match has two parts · the robot · the drive · driving it · knowing where it is · the program's shape · what actually changes.

🏁 First, what a competition actually feels like

A VEX match has two parts: an autonomous period, where the robot runs a routine entirely by itself, and then a driver-controlled period, where a driver takes over with the controller. Here's the thing — you already do both of these. Your Sumo robot's auto-hunt routine is autonomous; your tank driving is driver control. The qualifier squad does exactly the same two jobs, on a bigger field, with SpartanDrive. Everything below is just the vocabulary for those two jobs.

🧩 Why a "group" of one motor?

On your EXP base there's one motor per side, so a motor group looks like overkill. It isn't — it's a habit for the future. Competition V5 robots put two or three motors on each side, and the group is how you drive them as one. Learn the habit now on one motor; it scales without changing how you think.

Knowing where it is

This is where the built-in inertial sensor pays off — and where V5 reaches a little further.

The ideaA&R · VEX EXPTeam · SpartanDrive
Which way am I facing?Brain inertial → headingV5 IMU → drive.heading()
Where am I on the field? (x, y position)not tracked on EXPodometry → drive.get_pose()

The only genuinely new concept on the team is odometry — the robot tracking its own (x, y) spot on the field, not just its heading. Everything else you've already met.

What actually changes

To be straight with you: two things really are different, and pretending otherwise wouldn't help.

A&R · VEX EXPTeam · VEX V5
Where you write & run codeVEXcode (VS Code extension) → Download & RunPROS — a different editor setup and project layout
The C++ language & logicidentical — same thinking, same variables, loops, and if/else

So the editor and the build steps are new when you join the team. The way you think — name a device, command the drive, run a loop, sense and decide — does not change at all.

🏆 The one line to remember

Same C++ thinking, same robot concepts — a handful of methods get renamed and the editor changes when you reach the team. You're not starting over on V5. You're picking up where this course leaves off. Pull the one-page EXP → V5 cheat sheet when you make the jump.
